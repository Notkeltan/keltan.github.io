Why this room needs it

The office is upstairs, and its north wall is glass from the floor almost to the ceiling: about 5 m² of window on a 7.5 m² room. It passes 40°C in summer. I work from home in this room every day. SafeWork NSW puts the comfortable range for office work at 20 to 26°C.

It is damp as well as hot. A temperature and humidity sensor on my desk often reads above 50% humidity, and the highest reading so far is 73%.

Hot, damp, still air is how mould starts on paint, carpet and inside wardrobes. An air conditioner dries the air as it cools it, so this protects the room as well as the person working in it. It is a reverse-cycle unit, so it also heats the room in winter.

What gets installed

  • Indoor unit. A 2.5 kW reverse-cycle split system, high on the brick wall above the couch. The room is 2.86 m by 2.61 m, so the smallest size most brands sell is plenty.
  • Pipes. A slim cover runs above the window, tucked behind the blind track, then out through one 65 mm hole in the brick beside the window.
  • The shared wall is untouched. Nothing goes through the wall shared with next door.
  • Drain. The pipes go up and over the window, so a small pump beside the unit lifts the drain water.
  • Outdoor unit. On the balcony, at the end furthest from the neighbours.
  • Power. Its own circuit, installed by a licensed electrician.

How I chose the spot

I tested this with a computer simulation, not a real unit. Inside the 3D model of the room, a standard 2.5 kW indoor unit, 80 cm wide, was moved along both walls it could go on, 5 cm at a time: 77 positions. For each one the simulation checked whether the unit fits with the clearances manufacturers ask for, how far the air travels before it hits something, and how often it blows on a person at the desk or on the couch.

  • Brick wall above the couch: 0.96, the best of all 77. Air crosses the full width of the room and mostly passes beside the desk instead of landing on it.
  • Wall above the desk: 0.81 where a unit fits today, or 0.90 if I moved the plant off the tall shelf. Either way it sits over the screens, which skews its temperature sensor, and it blows at the couch.
  • Window wall and above the wardrobe: no room. The window wall is almost all glass, and there is a 33 cm gap above the wardrobe where a unit needs about 54 cm.

The brick wall has a price: a longer pipe run (about 6.5 m against 3.5 m) and the drain pump. Once pipe length is counted the two walls score about the same, 0.75 and 0.72. I'm choosing the better airflow and paying for the extra pipe. The run is well inside the 15 to 20 m that manufacturers allow for a unit this size.

Questions you might have

What if you move out early?

The unit stays either way. It belongs with the property from the day it goes in, whenever I leave.

Who pays if it breaks down?

I do, for as long as I live here. After that it is the owner's unit, with whatever remains of the manufacturer's warranty.

What does it do to the building?

One 65 mm hole through the brick beside the window, sealed around the pipes. A mounting plate fixed to the brick wall. A slim white pipe cover that mostly hides behind the blind track. A small outdoor unit standing on the balcony. Nothing goes through the wall shared with next door.

Will the neighbours hear it?

The outdoor unit goes at the end of the balcony furthest from the neighbours, with its fan facing out over the balcony. I'd mostly run it during the working day. NSW rules say an air conditioner must not be audible inside a neighbour's home between 10 pm and 7 am, or 8 am on weekends and public holidays, and I'd keep well clear of that.

Can the wiring handle it?

It gets its own circuit, installed and certified by a licensed electrician. A 2.5 kW unit draws about half a kilowatt while it runs, less than a kettle.

Why not a portable air conditioner?

I already use one, in my bedroom. A portable unit has to vent through an open door or window, it is louder, it can cost about twice as much to run as a split system of the same size, and when I leave it leaves with me. This one stays and adds to the property.

What happens after the 12 months?

We talk about the next lease in the usual way. The air conditioner stays where it is.
